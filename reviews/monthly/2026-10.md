## Executive Summary

The supplied October 2026 papers suggest a shift from isolated model capabilities toward integrated, verifiable AI systems. Agent research emphasizes reusable skills, environment design, selective learning and task-completion verification, while scientific AI extends foundation-model approaches to particle physics, electronic structure and medical imaging. Robotics and visual modeling increasingly combine learned representations with physical constraints, persistent state or executable rules. Evaluation also becomes more operational, addressing longitudinal clinical care, mobile-device constraints, data attribution, safety and energy costs. Across these areas, the central question is not simply whether models can produce convincing outputs, but whether their behavior remains grounded, reliable and efficient in realistic settings. ## Major Themes

- Agent engineering becomes a systems discipline: runtime harnesses, reusable interaction skills, training environments and verification receive attention alongside model capability.
- Scientific foundation models pursue broader reuse across tasks, materials and imaging modalities, often incorporating domain-specific structure.
- Grounded reasoning gains importance through explicit evidence representations, visual evidence access and analysis of what reasoning tokens actually reveal.
- Robotics connects semantic understanding to physical execution through simulation, force-aware control, tactile sensing and improved demonstration collection.
- World and visual models explore persistent spatial representations, structured memory and separation of executable dynamics from visual rendering.
- Efficiency research spans token allocation, attention, diffusion sampling, action generation and environment-level rollout scheduling.
- Evaluation moves toward practical validity, including clinical trajectories, device limitations, controlled attribution tests and leakage-resistant scientific benchmarks.
- Safety and governance extend beyond output filtering to instruction-data separation, enforceable human approval and unequal access to capable agents.

## Field Progress

- Agent research is developing more precise units of analysis: skills, environments, intermediate states and verification procedures. MiniWeb, SynthAPI, EnvSched and FOCUS represent complementary efforts to improve training and diagnose failures.
- Scientific AI shows broader scope for reusable representations, with contributions spanning collider events, charge densities, fluid dynamics and heterogeneous medical images. The CORNETO reusability report also emphasizes that robustness depends on data, priors and biological context.
- Generative and embodied systems increasingly make structure explicit. Executable game rules, latent spatial memory, symbolic priors and force-reactive control offer distinct ways to constrain otherwise flexible learned models.
- Evaluation increasingly accounts for deployment conditions and methodological validity. Mobile image tasks, longitudinal care, attribution controls, protein–ligand evaluation guidelines and energy accounting broaden what counts as useful performance.
- Research on reasoning and safety is becoming more mechanism-aware. The supplied perspectives caution against treating reasoning transcripts as faithful explanations, while attention-based defenses and approval protocols explore more explicit control mechanisms.

## Open Questions

- How reliably can active verification establish task completion when application state is only partially observable, and how should its cost scale with task risk?
- Which reasoning-token properties genuinely support monitoring, and how can evaluators distinguish informative traces from plausible but unfaithful explanations?
- How well do scientific foundation models transfer to new instruments, materials, institutions and operating conditions outside their training distributions?
- Can persistent spatial memory, symbolic structure and executable rules improve long-horizon consistency without making world models too expensive or inflexible?
- Do reported efficiency gains remain beneficial after accounting for training, verification, optimization overhead and end-to-end energy consumption?
- How can human-approval requirements and prompt-injection defenses remain effective across changing tools and adversarial environments, while access to capable agents remains unequal?

## Outlook

The next important tests are likely to concern system-level reliability rather than isolated capability demonstrations. Stronger evidence would combine controlled ablations, out-of-distribution evaluation, realistic task environments and full accounting of computational and oversight costs. Scientific and medical models particularly need validation that preserved information and transferable representations remain useful under deployment shifts. For agents and world models, explicit state, evidence and control mechanisms offer promising directions, but their benefits must be demonstrated over long interactions. The supplied collection points toward this agenda without establishing that its central reliability challenges have been solved.
